Duality interactive tool: Writing the Dual

Type any linear program: maximize or minimize, “≤”, “≥” or “=” constraints, and variables that are nonnegative, nonpositive or unrestricted in sign. Optionally rewrite it with valid operations. Then build the dual problem one step at a time, for the original and for your rewritten version, and check that both duals give the same answer.

1 · Duality cheat sheet

Read left → right when the primal is a maximization, right → left when it is a minimization. The dual of the dual is the primal.

Data moves. Objective coefficients ↔ right-hand sides. Coefficient matrix A ↔ its transpose AT.
Counts swap. One dual variable per primal constraint, and one dual constraint per primal variable.
Memory aid. In a max problem, “≤” constraints and “≥ 0” variables are the natural ones; in a min problem, “≥” and “≥ 0”. Natural ↔ natural, odd ↔ odd, “=” ↔ unrestricted.

2 · The primal problem

Any coefficient may be 0, including all objective coefficients or all right-hand sides. Coefficients accept integers, decimals or fractions such as 3, −1.5, 2/7. Choose each variable’s sign below its column.

3 · Rewrite the primal (optional)

Apply any valid operation, as many times as you like, or none at all. Each one gives an equivalent problem, so its dual must give the same answer.

4 · Write the dual, step by step

The dual is written below the primal, one piece at a time: press ▶ Play, or step with Next / Previous (also → / ←). The tabs switch between the original and the rewritten primal.

5 · Compare and check

The two duals look different but describe the same problem. An exact solver confirms the optimal values.
