Simplex method interactive tool: Big M Method

When the origin is not feasible (a “≥” or “=” constraint, or a negative right-hand side), the simplex method needs a starting basis. The Big M method builds one: standardize, make every right-hand side ≥ 0, add an artificial variable where a row has no usable slack, penalize each artificial with −M in the objective, remove the M’s from the artificial columns (pre-processing), then run the simplex tableau as usual.

1 · The problem

All decision variables are nonnegative. Constraints may be ≤, ≥ or =, and right-hand sides may be positive, negative or zero. Coefficients accept integers, decimals or fractions such as 3, −1.5, 2/7. Arithmetic is exact, and M is kept as a symbol.

3 · The Big M method at a glance

Building the Big M problem

  1. Step 1. Standard form. Make the objective a maximization. Add a slack (+) to each “≤” row and subtract a surplus (−) from each “≥” row. “=” rows stay as they are.
  2. Step 2. Right-hand sides ≥ 0. Multiply every row with a negative right-hand side by −1.
  3. Step 3. Artificial variables. A row whose slack has coefficient +1 already has a starting basic variable. Every other row (originally “≥”, “=”, or “≤” with a negative right-hand side) gets its own artificial variable x with coefficient +1.
  4. Step 4. Penalize. Maximize (original objective) − Mx − Mx − …, where M is a huge positive number.

Solving it

  1. Iteration 0. Write row (0): z − (objective) = 0, so each artificial column has +M in row (0). The artificials are basic, but their columns are not unit columns yet.
  2. Iteration 0′ (pre-processing). For each artificial basic in row (i): new row (0) = row (0) − M × row (i). Now every basic column is a unit column.
  3. Iterations 1, 2, …. Ordinary simplex tableau. Compare reduced costs by their M parts first (M dominates any number), then by the numbers.
  4. At the end. If an artificial variable is still basic with a positive value, the original problem is infeasible. Otherwise every artificial is 0 and the solution is optimal for the original problem.
