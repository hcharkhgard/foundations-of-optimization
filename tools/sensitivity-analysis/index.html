Sensitivity Analysis interactive tool: Tabular Form

Sensitivity analysis asks what happens to an optimal solution when the model changes, without solving the new model from scratch. Everything comes from the final simplex tableau: y* (row (0) under the slack columns) and S* (the constraint rows under the slack columns). The tool covers three questions: adding a new decision variable, re-optimizing after parameters change (Question 1), and the allowable range of a parameter (Question 2).

y*: row (0), slack columns S*: constraint rows, slack columns changed value or θ row of the left matrix · row being multiplied column of the right matrix · entering column entry or row being computed

1 · The problem

All variables are nonnegative and every constraint is “≤” with a nonnegative right-hand side, so the slacks give the starting basis and each constraint has its own slack column (that is where y* and S* are read). Coefficients accept integers, decimals or fractions such as 3, −1.5, 2/7. Arithmetic is exact.

2 · Step 0 · Solve the problem with the simplex method

Sensitivity analysis starts from the optimal tableau. From it we read y* and S*, the only information the three questions need.

3 · Sensitivity question

Pick a question, enter the change, then press Next in the bar at the bottom (or →). Back (←) undoes a step. While a calculation is playing, Space plays or pauses.

4 · Sensitivity analysis at a glance

Step 1 · the revised final tableau

Basic variablesS*AS*S*b
  1. Step 2. Use row operations so every basic variable again has a unit column (1 in its row, 0 elsewhere, including row (0)).
  2. Step 3. All reduced costs ≥ 0 and all RHS ≥ 0: optimal. Only a negative reduced cost: simplex. Only a negative RHS: dual simplex.

The three questions

  1. New variable. It adds one constraint to the dual. If y* satisfies it, the current solution stays optimal with the new variable = 0. If not, re-optimize with Steps 1–3.
  2. Question 1. After changing c, A or b: Steps 1, 2 and 3.
  3. Question 2. Write the parameter as (current value) + θ; do Steps 1 and 2 only. For bi: every RHS ≥ 0. For cj: every reduced cost ≥ 0. Solve for θ.
